Why Munea

Not one more chatbot, but the care gap finally filled

What buyers actually purchase isn't AI vocabulary. It's a service elders will use, front-line staff can read at a glance, and families can feel.

Companionship that remembers

Long-term memory lets every conversation pick up where the last one ended: a sore knee, a grandchild's wedding, the old street they love to visit. She keeps it all. Familiarity is what makes an elder open the app a second and third time.

Care that listens closely

In everyday conversation she picks up low mood and warning signs: a voice that sounds off, a mention of pain, something worrying said out loud. Family and caregivers are notified right away, so "something's not right" becomes "we knew early".

Peace of mind you can see

Family open the app and see how Mom and Dad are doing today, their mood, and whether reminders were completed. Worry from far away finally has somewhere to go, instead of one phone call after another.

Policy tailwind

2026 is a big year for care technology policy in Taiwan

Public funding that can go alongside adopting Munea:

  • Residential facility quality incentive program (2024–2027): backed by the Ministry of Health and Welfare's long-term care fund, with a "smart assistive care technology" indicator. Adopting a smart care solution can be written into your indicator plan, and we help prepare the paperwork.
  • Long-term Care 3.0 smart assistive device rental benefit (effective July 2026): NT$60,000 of rental allowance per care household every three years. Munea's application is in planning (a plan under review, not an established benefit item); if included, the cost to families should drop substantially.
Our principle: subsidies are a bonus, not a precondition. The value has to stand on its own first — hours given back to staff, reassurance for families, something employees actually feel. Subsidies just make the decision easier.

Boundaries and commitments

Care settings deserve an honest partner

Three things, stated up front:

Positioning

Assistive, not a replacement

Munea is an assistive care and reminder tool. It does not replace human care or emergency medical judgment, and it does not provide diagnosis or medical advice. The escalation flow and responsibilities for warning signs are defined explicitly in the contract.

Personal data

Data serves care, nothing else

Elders' conversations and health data are collected and processed under Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act, with consent forms, data processing agreements, retention limits and purpose limitation all in place. Data is never used for anything outside the service.

Language

Traditional Chinese for Taiwan

The service runs primarily in Mandarin, tuned to how people in Taiwan speak. Taiwanese Hokkien is still being improved, and we can assess your setting's language needs before rollout. Other languages are not supported today.
