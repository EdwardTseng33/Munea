ElliQ Alternatives: A Robot vs. a Phone App for Aging Parents

Munea Editorial Team·Updated November 18, 2026·7-minute read

In this guide

  2. The phone-app route
  3. Side-by-side comparison

If you are comparing ElliQ with other options, the biggest question is not which brand but which kind of product: a dedicated tabletop robot, or an app that runs on a phone. ElliQ is the robot. The alternatives are mostly apps that give your parent a voice companion without new hardware. Each suits a different parent and a different home.

This guide compares them fairly, using only what the companies publish. Everything below about ElliQ and other products was checked on their own pages in November 2026. Details change, so confirm with each company before you buy.

What ElliQ is

According to its official site, ElliQ is a voice-activated companion robot for older adults, made by Intuition Robotics. As of November 2026, the site describes the following.

  • Device: An all-in-one device, so no separate phone or computer is needed. It arrives pre-configured; the stated home requirements are power and Wi-Fi.
  • What it offers: Medication reminders, exercise suggestions, music, news and stories, trivia and games, photo and message sharing with loved ones, and group activities.
  • Family side: A caregiver app that lets family start a video call, send photos to ElliQ's display, set medication and hydration reminders, and see alerts if the member reports pain or a change in mood. The site also lists a view of when the member was last active. The primary caregiver gets full access, and other family members or trusted contacts can be invited.
  • What it is not: ElliQ states that it is not a medical device. For emergencies, it encourages members to contact their caregiver and trusted contacts. Its caregiver page calls it "a helping hand, not a replacement" for human care.
  • Cost model: The pages mention leasing, which includes hardware, software, updates and support, along with free shipping and home installation. The pages we could open show a monthly fee only as a placeholder with no amount, so the price is not published there. Ask ElliQ for current pricing.
  • Where it is sold: Countries and regions served are not published on the pages we reviewed.
  • Try it: The site offers a free online trial on a computer using Chrome or Safari.

One caution: the ElliQ pages we opened still showed an expected shipping notice of "End of 2019," which looks out of date. That is a reason to confirm availability and terms with the company directly.

The phone-app route

An app companion works on the phone your parent may already own. You trade the always-there presence of a robot for no new hardware, no installation, and a device that goes along when your parent leaves the house.

Munea

Munea is an iPhone app (iOS 15 or later). Your parent talks by voice with an AI character, Nina or Milo, who says plainly that it is an AI; there is no typing. It keeps the highlights of past chats, such as a family member's name, rather than raw recordings, and users can view or delete them. It sends medication and appointment reminders, and family members can join a family circle that shows the health and mood updates your parent chooses to share. Munea is free to download with 5 trial points (about 1 minute of conversation each); Plus is US$19.99 per month and Pro is US$39.99 per month, and prices shown in the app are the final word. Inviting family needs a paid plan (Plus up to 4 people, Pro up to 12, including the person who creates the circle).

What Munea does not do: it has no fall detection, no emergency button, no location, and no camera or sensors; it does not phone your parent; and it will not tell you something is wrong if the app is not opened. It is not a medical device. It is available on the App Store in the US, Canada, Taiwan and Japan, and not yet in the UK or Australia. There is no Android version.

EverFriends

EverFriends, made by EverFriends.ai, Inc., describes itself on its App Store page as "Video-based AI companions" for seniors and people experiencing loneliness or memory challenges. As of November 2026 the listing says it is for iPhone, requires iOS 16.6 or later, and is free to download with in-app purchases. The listed monthly plans range from US$14.99 to US$199.99 and include 60, 250, 750 and 1,200 minutes, with a 7-day free trial. The developer says the companions are not a substitute for professional medical care, therapists or emergency services.

Other voice companion apps exist, and new ones appear often. Whichever one you consider, read its App Store listing and privacy policy.

Which one suits your parent?

ElliQ may suit your parent better if:

  • They are home most of the day and would like a companion that is simply there on a table.
  • They don't use a smartphone comfortably, or you would rather they not have to.
  • You want a caregiver app with video calls, photo sending and alerts built around one dedicated device.
  • Your household is comfortable with a lease and a monthly fee.

A phone app may suit your parent better if:

  • They already use an iPhone for calls and photos.
  • You prefer no new hardware to buy, lease or install.
  • They go out often and would like their companion with them.
  • You want to try something for a few weeks before committing.

Neither is better in every home. Many families try one and learn what their parent likes.

How to decide

  1. Ask your parent. Show them a video or trial if possible. Their reaction matters more than any feature list.
  2. Check the home. A robot needs a good Wi-Fi connection and a spot to sit; an app needs a working phone.
  3. Read the costs. Ask for the full price, the minimum term, and how to cancel in writing.
  4. Read the privacy policy. Look at what is saved and who can see it. Our guide to AI companions for seniors lists questions to ask.
  5. Keep people in the picture. A companion should add to your calls and visits, not replace them. See how to check in on a parent who lives alone.

FAQ

What is the best alternative to ElliQ?

There is no single best one, because the right choice depends on your parent. If a tabletop robot is what you want, ElliQ is one option. If your parent uses an iPhone and you prefer no new hardware, a phone app such as Munea or EverFriends may fit. Compare cost, setup and what family can see.

Is ElliQ better than a phone app?

Not in every case. ElliQ is a dedicated device with a caregiver app, and it needs power and Wi-Fi at home. A phone app needs a phone and goes with your parent. A parent who is happy with a smartphone may like an app; one who is not may prefer a device.

Can an AI companion replace a caregiver or carer?

No. ElliQ, Munea and EverFriends all state that they are not medical or emergency services, and ElliQ calls itself a helping hand, not a replacement for human care. Think of any AI companion as company and routine for your parent, with family, neighbors and professionals still involved.
